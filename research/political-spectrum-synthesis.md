# Bangladesh Political Spectrum: Research Synthesis

## Status

Research hypothesis; not a verified classification of parties or media
outlets. Derived from the supplied “Bangladesh Political Spectrum Deep
Research” compilation and normalized for repository use.

## Strong hypotheses

- A conventional Western left–right line does not by itself describe
  Bangladesh's political competition.
- Partisan, geopolitical, socio-cultural, and governance orientations should
  be observed separately.
- The AL–BNP rivalry remains historically important, but Jamaat, NCP, Jatiya
  Party, left parties, and independent reform movements require explicit
  representation.
- Criticism of India may be nationalist, economic, strategic, secular, or
  religious and should not automatically imply any party affiliation.
- Constitutional secularism and Islam's status as state religion create a
  distinct socio-cultural and legal dimension.
- Media ownership is relevant but cannot substitute for content analysis.
- Outlet orientation can change after ownership, editorial, governmental, or
  major political transitions.
- Political bias and factual reliability are different constructs.

## Unresolved questions

- Which coding scale produces acceptable inter-coder agreement?
- What minimum article sample is representative across media formats?
- How should television talk shows be compared with print news and opinion?
- How quickly should historical ratings decay after editorial change?
- Which independent fact-checking sources provide sufficient, consistent
  coverage for a reliability metric?

## Excluded from verified data

The source compilation contains proposed numerical scores and strong
classifications for named outlets. These are not imported as facts because the
underlying evidence set, citations, sampling method, and reviewer agreement are
incomplete. All outlet records therefore begin as `unreviewed`.
