# Bangladesh Political Spectrum Framework

## Purpose

This framework describes dimensions that can be used to study political and
media discourse in Bangladesh. It is a coding framework, not a declaration
that any party, person, or news outlet belongs at a fixed point.

Ratings must be time-bounded, evidence-backed, independently reviewable, and
reported with uncertainty. Criticism of one political actor must not be
automatically treated as support for another.

## Four analytical axes

### 1. Partisan alignment

Bangladesh cannot be represented adequately by a single Awami League–BNP
line. Record evidence for each relevant actor independently:

- Awami League (AL)
- Bangladesh Nationalist Party (BNP)
- Bangladesh Jamaat-e-Islami
- National Citizen Party (NCP)
- Jatiya Party
- left and socialist parties
- other registered or emerging political groups
- independent, nonpartisan, or insufficient evidence

Scores on this axis are not required to sum to one. An outlet can be critical
of several parties, neutral toward others, or change over time.

### 2. Geopolitical framing

Measure how coverage frames Bangladesh's relationships with India and other
foreign powers. Keep distinct:

- support for cooperation with India;
- criticism of particular Indian policies;
- broader claims about Indian influence or hegemony;
- support for a balanced or sovereignty-centered foreign policy;
- orientation toward China, Pakistan, the United States, or other partners.

Do not infer religious or partisan alignment from criticism of India.

### 3. Socio-cultural orientation

Code observable treatment of:

- constitutional secularism and Article 2A;
- religious freedom and pluralism;
- Islamist political participation;
- minority rights;
- gender and social policy;
- Bengali and Bangladeshi national identity.

Suggested descriptive positions are secular-liberal, pluralist/moderate, and
Islamist-conservative. These are analytical labels, not judgments of civic
worth or factual reliability.

### 4. Governance orientation

Measure support for:

- the incumbent government or existing power structure;
- competitive elections and institutional checks;
- anti-authoritarian or anti-corruption reform;
- constitutional or systemic change;
- restoration of an earlier party-led political order.

This axis helps distinguish principled criticism of incumbents from support
for an opposition party.

## Scoring principles

1. Store observations before calculating labels.
2. Use a defined review window and minimum evidence count.
3. Keep historical and current assessments separate.
4. Record confidence and disagreement between coders.
5. Treat ownership as one signal, not proof of editorial behavior.
6. Separate political orientation from factual reliability.
7. Publish an `unreviewed` or `insufficient_evidence` state instead of forcing
   a label.

## Prohibited shortcuts

- Do not derive an outlet's position from a single article.
- Do not convert government criticism into an opposition-party score.
- Do not convert secularism into support for the Awami League.
- Do not convert religious conservatism into support for BNP or Jamaat.
- Do not treat an AI-generated score, public reputation, boycott, attack, or
  government sanction as conclusive evidence.
- Do not use loaded descriptions as dataset facts unless they are clearly
  attributed quotations from cited sources.

## Versioning

Every published assessment must include the methodology version, assessment
period, evidence count, reviewer status, and last-reviewed date. Major changes
to an axis or label definition require a new methodology version.
